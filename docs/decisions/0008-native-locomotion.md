# ADR 0008: Ground-gated movement assistance and procedural gait

Date: 2026-11-24. Status: accepted for native flat-ground fixtures. Specification §§11.3, 12, 26, 29. Extends [standing control](0007-support-aware-standing.md).

## Decision

`LocomotionRagdoll` owns the existing `PoseRagdoll` and a small walking controller. `gait.rs` generates relative joint targets; `walking_controller.rs` samples support and computes bounded commands; `locomotion_config.rs` validates versioned tuning. No renderer, transport, wall clock or transform override enters this boundary. `PoseRagdoll::step()` still targets the bind pose; the locomotion owner supplies changing targets through an internal step method.

World-space X/Z input is finite, normalized to a unit disk without overflowing on maximum finite floats, and retains analog magnitudes below one. Running selects a different speed/cadence. Input persists until replaced; invalid input preserves the previous intent. Reset discards intent, queued external impulses, phase, braking, strength and diagnostics while preserving the enable setting. Disabling clears movement intent/controller state and restores idle gains, but preserves queued external impulses so physical shoves still apply to the disabled ragdoll. The wrapper samples queued departures before support or movement assistance.

## Linear command and application points

Support uses the standing controller's current-shape contact qualification: external nonsensor contacts, upward normal, small distance, and foot/surface normal separation speed ≤0.5 m/s. The selected contact is the most upward, then closest, with collider-handle order breaking exact ties. Each foot reports surface velocity at the actual contact, including angular motion. Two supported feet contribute equally to the reference velocity. Friction is read from Rapier's solver contacts, using the least coefficient among eligible manifold contacts for that pair; it includes Rapier's material combination rule.

The active desired world velocity is `input × selected_speed + support_velocity`. Acceleration follows the mass-weighted character velocity with response time 0.05 s, removes Y, and clamps its whole-vector magnitude to 12 m/s². There is no integrated velocity error, desired-position tether, vertical lift or per-body duplication of the acceleration budget. With the 40 kg rig, the maximum total command is **8 N·s per tick**. Speed values are controller targets, not clamps on velocity generated by impacts or contacts.

Applying the entire command at all body centers caused forward tipping as feet dragged. Applying it entirely to the feet caused backward tipping. The final controller divides the same command between mass-proportional body impulses and the currently supported feet:

```text
direction = normalized horizontal acceleration, or zero
lean = pelvis_up · direction
lean_rate = pelvis_angular_velocity · (world_up × direction)
fraction = clamp(mean_support_friction × |gravity_y| / max(|acceleration|, 0.001)
                 + drive_tilt_gain × lean + drive_tilt_damping_s × lean_rate, 0, 1)
body_impulse_i = mass_i × acceleration × dt × (1 − fraction)
foot_impulse_j = total_mass × acceleration × dt × fraction / supported_foot_count
```

The body's own mass-proportional term also applies to each foot. All terms point in the same commanded direction, so both the net impulse and sum of impulse magnitudes retain the single budget. Moving the application points deliberately changes angular response; this is not a torque-free actuator. No equal/opposite force is applied to the support surface: this is **ground-gated game movement assistance**, not a traction-limited leg model. Zero-friction support still permits a mass-distributed command. The current controller is calibrated on a horizontal surface; its friction estimate is not a slope-force model.

No foot support, or pelvis-up Y ≤0.5, removes linear/yaw assistance. Relative pose control can remain while strength relaxes. Repeated input against a wall never accumulates a later burst from integral error. Contact physics can still block or topple the rig.

## Gait, heading and motor strength

The legs use alternating half-cycle stance sweeps and raised returns. During stance, the hip sweeps forward to backward with an extended knee. During return, the hip sweeps back to front while knee flexion peaks midway and returns to zero before touchdown. Half of knee flexion is offset at the hip and ankle, lifting the foot beneath the body. Target angles are clamped to authored per-axis ranges. A 0.15 s blend approaches input magnitude; cadence is 1.6 Hz walking or 2.5 Hz running. Phase wraps deterministically at the fixed 60 Hz step. Support gates gait progression; this is not terrain-aware touchdown scheduling or a no-slip foot-plant constraint.

Heading uses a separate supported pelvis yaw actuator, with stiffness 50 N·m/rad, damping 5 N·m·s/rad and cap 20 N·m. Zero movement input adds no heading torque. The existing balance tilt cap remains 30 N·m with two feet, 15 with one, and zero airborne. These are independent yaw and tilt budgets; their vector sum can exceed 30 N·m.

`config/locomotion-motors.json` strengthens only thighs, shins and ankles to stiffness 600 N·m/rad, damping 12 N·m·s/rad, and vector cap 150 N·m. The existing conservative per-free-axis division still applies. Loaded stance/swing tests collapsed with the standing leg preset, motivating this separate asset. Arms, spine, neck and fixed wrists keep standing values. Motor schema 1 accepts up to 600 stiffness and 150 torque for `implicit_velocity_pd`; explicit-PD ceilings stay 200/50. Both retain damping ceiling 20 and unactuated wrists. Accepted tuning is bounded, not a promise that every combination is stable.

Holding movement or braking uses the active preset. Release starts the bundled 60-tick braking interval (the schema ceiling is 120 ticks); repeated zero input does not restart that timer. Then weaker COM velocity damping uses response 0.5 s, acceleration cap 1 m/s², and mass-proportional application only. It removes residual sway without storing a position to return to. Active joint strength relaxes toward the original standing preset over 0.5 s of eligible settled ticks: relative COM speed <0.15 m/s, pelvis spin <0.3 rad/s, and pelvis-up Y >0.98. Unsupported or fallen poses also relax. Relaxation pauses if the supported rig becomes unsettled. Impossible strict endpoints are rejected by configuration validation. Disabled/reset state immediately restores idle gains. The settled idle fixture still yields to the original three 10 N·s chest impacts.

## Consequences and sources

This supplies native assisted walking/running with finite effort and observable gait, not a complete character controller. Slopes, steps, moving-surface transitions, rapid reversals, loaded grips, combat interruptions, browser/WASM locomotion parity and human assessment remain required. Stronger legs may affect fighting and entanglement; those interactions require their own gates. Existing standing/passive browser behavior is separately regression-tested. No locomotion option is exposed in the browser yet.

Rapier documents the distinction between persistent forces and tick-scoped impulses in its [force/impulse guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/rigid_body_forces_and_impulses/). Implementation was checked against pinned Rapier 0.26.1 owning sources: `dynamics/rigid_body.rs` (`apply_impulse`, `velocity_at_point`, `kinetic_energy`), `dynamics/coefficient_combine_rule.rs`, and `geometry/contact_pair.rs` (`SolverContact::friction`). Momentum, material-rule, moving-platform and wall-energy tests verify these uses. [Slice evidence](../slices/06a-native-locomotion.md) records the test scope and remaining gates.

Browser integration was subsequently verified in [slice 06b](../slices/06b-browser-locomotion.md). [ADR 0009](0009-browser-locomotion.md) records the explicit deterministic-math calls required by its native/WASM trace and the diagnostic input/target contracts. Terrain and human acceptance remain open.
